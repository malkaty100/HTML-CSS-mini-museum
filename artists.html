Vincent van Gogh

1853-1890

Vincent van Gogh was a Dutch post-impressionist painter whose unique style made him one of the most famous artists in history. He was known for his vivid colors, energetic brushstrokes, and expressive paintings. Van Gogh often painted landscapes, flowers, and everyday scenes, transforming ordinary subjects into powerful works of art. Although he struggled to gain recognition during his lifetime, his paintings later became enormously influential. Today, his works are admired around the world for their emotion, movement, and distinctive visual style. Famous Works: The Starry Night, Sunflowers, Irises

Leonardo da Vinci

1452-1519

Leonardo da Vinci was an Italian Renaissance artist, inventor, scientist, and thinker who combined artistic creativity with a deep curiosity about the world. He carefully studied nature, anatomy, light, and human expression, using his observations to improve his paintings. His works are famous for their extraordinary detail, realistic figures, and mysterious atmosphere. Leonardo is considered one of the greatest figures of the Renaissance and an outstanding example of the connection between art, science, and innovation. Famous Works: Mona Lisa, The Last Supper, Lady with an Ermine

Claude Monet

1840–1926

Claude Monet was a French painter and one of the leading figures of the Impressionist movement. He was fascinated by light, color, nature, and the way a scene could change throughout the day. Instead of focusing on precise details, Monet often used loose brushstrokes and layers of color to capture a particular moment and atmosphere. He frequently painted gardens, landscapes, rivers, and scenes from everyday life. His famous series of paintings helped establish Impressionism as one of the most important movements in modern art. Famous Works: Water Lilies, Impression, Sunrise, Woman with a Parasol
